# Primer: Shawne Merriman on Bussin' With The Boys

## What this episode is

*Bussin' With The Boys* is a loose, joke-heavy football podcast. The hosts are Will Compton (a former linebacker) and Taylor Lewan (a former offensive lineman), both ex-NFL. They talk like teammates in a locker room, so expect profanity, trash talk, and tangents. This is a "Best of the Bus" rerun of a guest episode.

The guest is Shawne Merriman, a star pass rusher from the mid-2000s. His nickname is "Lights Out." The episode covers his football career, his suspension, and what he does now. The hosts assume you know a handful of names and ideas, so here they are.

## The football basics

**Pass rusher / outside linebacker.** Merriman's job was to sprint at the quarterback and tackle him for a **sack**. Sacks are the headline stat for the position. Double digits in a season is very good, and 15+ is elite.

**Linebacker shorthand.** The hosts toss around "Sam," "Mike," "strong side," and "weak side." These are linebacker positions and labels. The "strong side" is the side of the field where the offense has more players, such as an extra tight end. The call from the middle linebacker tells each player whether to rush the passer or drop back into pass coverage. Learning that system is hard for a rookie.

**Oklahoma drill.** An old-school, one-on-one collision drill. Two players face off in a small space, and everyone watches. The hosts talk about it as a test of toughness.

**Fullbacks.** A fullback is a big blocker who clears the way for the running back. Fullbacks are among the hardest players to rush past. One of Merriman's teammates, Lorenzo Neal, was famous at the position.

## The Chargers of that era

The Chargers played in San Diego until 2017, when they moved to Los Angeles. In the mid-2000s they were one of the best teams in the league, but they never won a championship. Names the hosts drop:

- **LaDainian Tomlinson ("LT")**: the star running back.
- **Philip Rivers**: the quarterback, famous for nonstop trash talk. He starred at North Carolina State in college.
- **Antonio Gates**: a star tight end.
- **Drew Brees**: the starting quarterback early on, before Rivers took over.
- **Marty Schottenheimer**: the head coach, a hard-nosed old-school type.
- **A.J. Smith**: the general manager.
- **Wade Phillips, Greg Manusky, John Pagano**: defensive coaches.

The defensive scheme was a **3-4**: three linemen and four linebackers. It lets linebackers like Merriman rush from the edge.

## The draft and contracts

**Draft order.** Teams pick in order of worst record to best. Players often "fall" past where they were projected. Merriman's draft talk references the 2005 draft, where many teams chose ahead of the Chargers. Some of the names you'll hear:

- Alex Smith (49ers, first pick)
- Braylon Edwards (Browns, third pick)
- Carlos Rogers (Redskins)
- DeMarcus Ware (Cowboys)

**Old CBA.** The CBA is the collective bargaining agreement, the contract between the league and the players' union. Before 2011, rookie contracts were negotiated one by one and could drag on, so rookies sometimes missed camp. A 2011 deal set a fixed pay scale for rookies. When Merriman says "old CBA," he means that earlier era.

**Waivers.** When a team cuts a player, other teams can "claim" him. The team with the worst record gets the first shot. That is why a very bad team can end up with a cut veteran.

**Rookie dinner.** An NFL hazing tradition. Rookies, usually the highest draft picks, pay for an expensive team dinner. Bills of several thousand dollars are normal. Anything much higher is considered a legend.

## The PED suspension

This is the part the episode's title flags, so the background matters.

**PEDs** are performance-enhancing drugs, such as steroids and stimulants. The NFL tests players randomly, including in the offseason. It keeps a **banned substance list**, and it holds players to **strict liability**: if a banned substance is in your system, you're responsible, even if it came from a supplement you didn't know was banned. A first offense in that era meant a four-game suspension. Supplements are loosely regulated, so contaminated or mislabeled products are a real problem. **NSF Certified for Sport** is a label meant to show a product was independently screened.

**BALCO.** The hosts mention this scandal, so here's the quick version. BALCO was a California lab that supplied designer steroids to elite athletes in the early 2000s. It was tied to baseball star Barry Bonds and Olympic sprinter Marion Jones. The scandal made PED news huge around the same time Merriman came into the league. Mark McGwire and Sammy Sosa are other names from that baseball steroid era.

**Pro Bowl.** The league's annual all-star game. Fans, players, and coaches vote on who plays. It was held in Hawaii in those years. Taylor Lewan also served a PED suspension himself, which is why he jumps in with his own story.

Merriman moved into business and media, so the second half covers some industry basics.

**MMA and promotions.** **MMA** is mixed martial arts. The **UFC** is the biggest league. Smaller leagues like **Bellator** exist below it, and **WWE** is scripted pro wrestling. A **feeder league** is a smaller league that develops fighters and sends the best ones up to the UFC. Merriman started his own promotion, **Lights Out Extreme Fighting**. Jay Glazer (a Fox Sports reporter who runs an MMA gym) and Randy Couture (a UFC legend) are names from his training days.

**Jake Paul vs. Mike Tyson.** This episode was recorded in early November 2024, just before the fight. Paul is a YouTuber turned boxer in his late 20s. Tyson is a former heavyweight champion in his late 50s. There was a lot of debate about whether it would be a real fight.

**Streaming and FAST channels.** **FAST** stands for free ad-supported streaming TV. Think of live-style channels you can watch for free, with commercials. **Pluto TV** was an early FAST service, and Paramount bought it in 2019. # Primer: Shawne "Lights Out" Merriman and the World Around Him

## Who's on the bus

Expect locker-room language, constant teasing, and a recurring segment or two (a hypothetical "twisted question," and "tier talk," where people rank things).

**Shawne Merriman** is the guest. He grew up in Prince George's County, Maryland, just outside Washington, D.C. Locals call the D.C.–Maryland–Virginia area "the DMV." He played college ball at the University of Maryland. In 2005 the San Diego Chargers drafted him 12th overall. For a few seasons he was one of the most feared defenders in football. His nickname, "Lights Out," came with a signature celebration. Injuries later cut his career short. He finished with a brief stint on the Buffalo Bills and retired around 2012.

## The position he played

Football has 11 players per side. Some terms you'll hear:

- **Linebacker**: a defender who lines up a few yards behind the big linemen. Linebackers both tackle runners and cover receivers.
  - Teams name them by role: **Sam** (strong side), **Mike** (middle), **Will** (weak side).
  - The "strong side" is whichever side the offense has an extra blocker on, usually the **tight end**. A tight end is a big player who can both block and catch passes.
- **Pass rusher / edge rusher**: a defender whose main job is to charge into the backfield and tackle the quarterback.
- **Sack**: tackling the quarterback before he throws. It's the stat pass rushers are judged by.
  - Rough scale: 10 sacks in a season is very good, and 15+ is elite.
  - Merriman had about 40 in his first three seasons, which is a historic pace.
- **Blitz vs. drop**: on each play, a linebacker either rushes the quarterback (blitz) or backs up to cover an area of the field (drop into coverage). "Curl," "flat," and "hook" are names for those areas.
- **Fullback**: a stocky blocker who lines up in the backfield and clears a path for the running back.
- **Defensive end**: a lineman on the end of the defensive line. Scouts sometimes debate whether a big, fast prospect should be an end or a linebacker.

## How players get into (and out of) the NFL

- **The draft**: each spring, teams take turns picking college players. Generally, the worse your record, the earlier you pick.
  - "First-rounder" means one of the top ~32 picks. These players get the biggest contracts and the most hype.
  - Before the draft, teams invite prospects for visits (the "top 30" visits) and interviews.
- **Holdout**: a drafted player who skips practice until his contract is finalized.
- **The CBA** (collective bargaining agreement) is the labor deal between the league and the players' union, the **NFLPA**. It sets rules on pay, practice time, and drug testing. The rules changed a lot in 2011. When someone says "old CBA," they mean the looser pre-2011 rules.
- **Waived / waivers**: when a team cuts a player, other teams can claim him. The team with the worst record gets first dibs.
- **Vested veteran**: a player with enough seasons (around four) to earn certain rights and status.
- **Honors**:
  - **Pro Bowl**: the all-star game. It used to be held in Hawaii.
  - **All-Pro**: a more exclusive "best at your position" list.
  - **Defensive Player of the Year**: the top individual award for a defender.
  - **Hall of Fame**: the permanent honor. A "gold jacket" is what inductees receive.

## Football culture you'll hear about

- **Training camp**: grueling summer practices before the season.
- **One-on-ones**: practice drills that pit one blocker against one rusher.
- **Oklahoma drill**: a brutal old-school drill where two players collide head-on while teammates circle around and watch.
- **Film**: game footage teams study in meetings, sometimes to praise players and sometimes to embarrass them.
- **Rookie hazing**: first-year players carry veterans' gear and do chores. A famous ritual is the **rookie dinner**, where veterans order absurdly expensive food and drinks and stick a rookie with the bill. **Louis XIII** is a cognac that can cost thousands of dollars a bottle.
- **Stinger**: a nerve injury from a hard hit that sends burning numbness down the arm.
- **Achilles tear**: a rupture of the big tendon above the heel. It's devastating for players who rely on explosive speed.

## Steroids, PEDs, and the BALCO era

**PEDs** are performance-enhancing drugs, such as anabolic steroids. In the early-to-mid 2000s, a scandal centered on **BALCO**, a California lab run by Victor Conte, linked steroids to track stars and baseball sluggers like **Barry Bonds**. Mark McGwire and Sammy Sosa faced suspicion around the same time. Congress held hearings. Public anger at "cheaters" was intense.

In 2006, Merriman was suspended four games after testing positive for a banned substance. The episode title flags this.

The NFL's drug policy works on **strict liability**: whatever is in your body is your responsibility, even if it came from a contaminated over-the-counter supplement. That's why players care about labels like **NSF Certified for Sport**, which marks supplements tested for banned ingredients. Even so, a positive test still counts. The suspension also sparked public debate about whether players suspended for PEDs should be eligible for awards and honors that season.
