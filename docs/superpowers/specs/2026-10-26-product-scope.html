Why

Consuming isn't compounding

Years of podcasts and newsletters haven't added up. Every episode feels new because there's nothing for it to attach to. The problem is integration, not consumption. The target is level 2 across the subjects I choose.

LevelWhat it meansExample: a company
1 · ForeignThe vocabulary itself is unfamiliar"Monitoring software." What does that even mean?
2 · ConversationalThe target. Vocabulary, how it works, the players, rough numbers, live debates, and a take of my ownI've seen the product and know what it does and why people buy it
3 · ExpertRequires hands-on work and the knowledge that only comes from doing itI've integrated it myself and know its quirks

Getting content in

Three ways in:

Processing

What happens to every source

Every source I consume goes through the same steps, in order. Primers are always built but only pushed to me sometimes (see Today). Cards are generated no matter what I already know, and reviews take care of the rest.

  1. Get the textTranscript, article or notes
  2. SummarizeGist, key ideas, numbers with timestamps
  3. CategorizeDomain → topic, creating the topic if needed
  4. TagTopics and key concepts
  5. Build a primerThe background it assumes I have
  6. Generate cards3–5 cards on concepts, mechanisms and rough numbers
  7. LinkTo related items already in the library

Inside Today · Pulse

The two-minute world update

This replaces my current habit of letting newsletters pile up and then asking Claude for a summary.

  • Grouped by story, not by newsletter. Five newsletters covering the same story become one item.
  • Ranked by importance (how widely covered and how big a deal) and by relevance to my domains. Noise is dropped.
  • Top 5–8 stories, each with what happened and why it matters. Everything else collapses under "Also happening."
  • Covers everything since I last checked. Skipping three days produces one brief, not three, and it always takes about two minutes.
  • Go deeper queues the full piece, or opens it as a consumed library item with full processing.
  • Weekly news quiz: a few short cards on the week's stories. They expire instead of entering long-term review.
  • No notification. The brief waits in Today until I get to it.

Tab 4 of 5 · Review

Recall it, don't recognize it

  • Open answers only, typed or spoken. Multiple choice gives away the answer.
  • Graded on the idea, not the wording: got it / partly / missed. A fast model with fixed answer choices, like Jev, looks like a good fit.
  • When the grader isn't confident, it shows the model answer and I grade myself. I can always override with "actually, I knew that."
  • Partly and missed get a short explanation of what was missing.
  • Tell Carl: after any grade I can say what I actually care about. That rewrites the card's accepted answer and shapes future cards.
  • "Don't care" deletes the card and teaches Carl what not to generate.
  • Spaced repetition (e.g. FSRS): fast, correct answers mean I see the card less often.
  • Session order: foundation cards from primers first, then mechanisms, then rough numbers. No trivia.
  • Calibrate before launch: use ~10 examples from my imported notes, mark what I would actually want quizzed, and tune generation until it matches.
