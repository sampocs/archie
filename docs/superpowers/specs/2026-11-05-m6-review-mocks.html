M6 Cards + review · draft for review

Daily review, and the questions it raises

The core review screen is already designed in the UX spec. You answer under the question, and the text box turns into the graded card. Drop sits at the bottom-left. Shake to undo. These mocks redraw it in the app's current style, and then focus on the parts the spec left open: grading when the model isn't sure or you're offline, what "keep going" looks like, cards on item pages, the weekly news quiz, and the daily reminder. Every open choice is listed in the numbered boxes with my recommendation in bold.

01

Today, answering, graded

The review card sits on Today in the forest review colour. It counts due cards, gives a time estimate and says how many foundation cards come first. You answer by typing or with the mic. On submit the box turns into the graded card: what you said, the verdict, what was missing, and the full answer. Below it are "I actually knew it" and "What I care about…".

Decisions
  1. Next is a button, not automatic. You read the grade, then tap Next. An automatic advance after "Got it" feels faster, but you lose the chance to read the full answer.
  2. Voice uses on-device dictation. It works offline and costs nothing. Whisper through Groq would be more accurate on names, but it needs a connection.
  3. Session order follows the product spec: foundations first, then mechanisms, then rough numbers, and inside each group, the most overdue first.
02

When the model isn't sure, and when you're offline

Grading needs a connection, but review must work on a plane. In both cases you see the answer and grade yourself with the same three buttons.

Decisions
  1. Offline, you grade yourself and the card is scheduled right away. The alternative is to save your typed answer and grade it later, when you're back online. That keeps grading consistent, but it delays scheduling, and the "what was missing" note would arrive long after the card.
  2. The grader is Haiku with one worked example, from the spike (spikes/card_grading/README.md). It scored 97.6% on 124 labelled answers, every miss was one step off, a grade takes about 1.0s typically and 1.4s at worst, and it costs about $0.0001. Sonnet was only slightly more accurate (98.4%) but took up to 3.9s.
  3. "Not sure" is triggered by time, not by the model. No model ever admitted being unsure in the spike, so the self-grade screen appears when grading takes longer than about 3 seconds or fails. A self-grade button also stays one tap away under every grade ("I actually knew it").
03

Done, and keeping going

The done screen shows the tally, then offers more: review ahead (cards due in the next few days) or practise one topic or source. The Review tab opens to the same choices when nothing is due.

Decisions
  1. Review ahead and topic practice both count toward scheduling, with a lighter effect for cards that weren't due yet. The other option is that practice never changes schedules. That's simpler, but it wastes the effort.
  2. No daily limit on new cards for now. Your library is small. We add a limit once a day ever exceeds about 40 cards.
04

Cards on an item, the news quiz, the reminder

A finished item lists its cards with when each is next due. The weekly news quiz is a few cards made from the week's brief stories, offered on Today; quiz cards expire instead of joining long-term review. Settings get one reminder time.

Decisions
  1. Quiz on Sunday, 5 cards from the week's top stories, expiring after 7 days. It's built from the stories you marked Done in briefs that week.
  2. One reminder a day at 8:30pm, only if cards are due, with the time changeable in Settings. The notification reads "12 cards · about 4 min".
  3. Editing a card stays out of M6. "What I care about…" already rewrites the answer, and Drop removes a card.
  4. "What I care about…" rewrites only this card's answer and saves the note as a preference for future cards. It doesn't rewrite your other cards.
